faqs:
  - q: "I've been to the hospital emergency room and my medical doctor. Do I really need to see a chiropractor?"
    a: |
      You need a doctor who understands car accident trauma and can diagnose and treat auto accident injuries. Hospital emergency rooms and other emergency services check your vital signs, make sure there are no life-threatening conditions and release you with pain medication. They don't treat the underlying injuries that often lead to pain later.

      ER and medical doctors are not set up to manage ongoing car accident injuries. That is why we suggest making an appointment with our office right after your hospital visit. Our office is dedicated to auto accident patients and uses forms of care designed to correct structural damage.
  - q: "I didn't go to the emergency room or my doctor. Do you offer emergency car accident care, and when can I get an appointment?"
    a: |
      Yes. You can get emergency car accident treatment at our office even if you haven't seen a medical doctor or gone to the hospital. We have same-day appointments available. Give us a call and we will see you today.
  - q: "Should I see an attorney before I see my chiropractor?"
    a: |
      We believe that if you have been in a car crash of any type, you should hire an attorney. It is just as important to get examined by a doctor as soon as possible after the accident.

      Your exam findings need to be documented right after the accident by a car accident chiropractor to decide what care you need for the best recovery. Your attorney will also want to know what injuries you have.
  - q: "What if my car sustained only minor damage?"
    a: |
      Studies by car crash experts Arthur Croft, DC, Charles Carroll, MD, Paul McAtee, MD and Lee Riley, MD found that "the amount of damage to the automobile bears little relation to the force applied to the cervical spine (neck) of the occupants."

      In other words, the injuries to the people in a car are not directly related to the damage to the car. That is why every auto accident injury should be evaluated right away by a qualified neck and back chiropractor.
  - q: "What if I need an MRI or need to see an MD after you examine me?"
    a: |
      We will refer you right away for an MRI or any other imaging that is medically necessary to evaluate your injuries. We refer to facilities that work on a lien, so you don't have to pay up front for those studies.

      We also work with medical doctors who understand the personal injury process, who also work on a lien and wait for payment until your case settles.
  - q: "If I need chiropractic care, do I have to pay for it myself?"
    a: |
      If you are not at fault, your care is typically covered in full, with no out-of-pocket cost to you. We will help you get the costs of your car accident care covered.
  - q: "The insurance company has been calling me and wants to settle. What should I do?"
    a: |
      This is exactly why we strongly suggest hiring an attorney. Don't sign or settle anything until we have evaluated your injury. If you settle with the insurance company before your injury has fully healed, you will be on your own and will have to pay for any further care yourself.
  - q: "My insurance adjuster said I don't need a chiropractor because it was a low-impact accident. Is that true?"
    a: |
      No. Your insurance adjuster cannot decide whether you need care or what type of care to get. Adjusters are trained to keep accident costs to a minimum, and that applies to your body as well as your car. The amount of damage to your car often doesn't match the amount of injury to your back, neck and spine.

      Even crashes at very low speeds in Arizona can seriously injure your body; crash studies show that many injuries happen in accidents under 20 miles per hour. Even if you don't feel pain right away, it is smart to get a spinal checkup after a car accident to find any injury you may not know about.

      There are wrecks every day in the Phoenix area, some major and some minor, and many leave people injured, sometimes for the rest of their lives. Many car accident victims in Arizona think they are fine because they don't feel pain at first. But no pain does not mean no injury. Almost every crash jolts the body to some degree, and the body's fight-or-flight response can block pain at first. Some people feel pain the next day; for others it can take weeks or months.
