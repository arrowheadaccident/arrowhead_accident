## Headaches after a car accident

Headaches can begin immediately after a crash or appear hours or days later. They may be associated with a [whiplash injury](/whiplash-injury/) or a head injury. An examination is needed to assess the cause; the timing of a headache alone does not show how serious an injury is.

## Why prompt assessment matters

Tell a medical professional about every symptom, including any change in pain, concentration, memory, sleep or balance. Prompt assessment helps guide treatment, and accurate records document your symptoms and care.

If a headache worsens and does not go away, or you have repeated vomiting, seizures, weakness, slurred speech or difficulty staying awake, call 911 or go to an emergency department. These are among the [warning signs described by the CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html).

## Head injuries can occur without a direct blow

A sudden jolt to the body can move the head and brain rapidly, even if your head does not strike anything. A closed head injury is a nonpenetrating injury; it does not necessarily mean that there is no skull fracture. A traumatic brain injury (TBI) affects how the brain works, and a concussion is a mild TBI.

## Symptoms and diagnosis

Symptoms may include headaches, dizziness, fatigue, sensitivity to light or noise, memory or concentration problems, mood changes and sleep disturbances. Symptoms and their severity vary between people.

A normal scan does not rule out a concussion. A medical professional assesses symptoms and examination findings and decides whether imaging or specialist care is needed. Chiropractic care for neck pain does not replace medical assessment of a possible brain injury.
