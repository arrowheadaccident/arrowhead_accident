## Headaches are a very common symptom after a car accident

Headaches are especially common when the accident caused an injury to the head or a whiplash injury. Head pain after a car accident is really quite common, and it does not always start right away.

Head pain can begin immediately after a car accident injury; other times headaches are delayed by a few hours or even days. Many symptoms can be delayed, and the fourth or fifth day is often the worst day for delayed symptoms of all kinds, including headaches. Either way, it is crucial to get the medical and chiropractic treatment you need as quickly as possible, and to tell the treating physician or personal injury chiropractor about every symptom you have, especially headaches.

## Why prompt treatment and documentation matter

Prompt medical and chiropractic treatment for a headache after an auto accident has two benefits.

1. **It protects your health.** It increases the chances that doctors will find the cause of the headache and be able to treat it, which reduces the risk of long-term disability from head pain, closed head injury, traumatic brain injury or other injuries like whiplash.
2. **It protects your legal rights.** A diagnosed and documented headache helps protect your right to be compensated for medical losses after a car accident. The sooner your headaches and other injuries are documented, the easier it is to show that the accident caused them, and the harder it is for an insurance company to claim that your treatment was unrelated to the accident and therefore unnecessary.

## Headaches can happen even without a blow to the head

Car accident headache symptoms can appear even if your head was not struck. If you did hit your head on the steering wheel or any other part of the car, you may have suffered head trauma, and that needs to be evaluated right away whether or not you had a headache at the scene. Frequent, constant or chronic headaches after a car accident can be a sign of migraine headaches or even a closed head injury, also called a traumatic brain injury.

Traumatic brain injuries (TBI) are a leading cause of disability and even death in the United States, and motor vehicle accidents are one of the leading causes of closed head injuries.

"Closed head injury" means there is no fracture of the skull. The danger is that closed head injuries are often not diagnosed at first and usually do not lead to a hospital stay, because many auto accident victims think the symptoms will just go away, or don't connect a delayed headache with the accident.

## What exactly is a traumatic brain injury (TBI)?

Here is a short definition from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traumatic_brain_injury):

> TBI is a major cause of death and disability worldwide, especially in children and young adults. Males sustain traumatic brain injuries more frequently than do females. Causes include falls, vehicle accidents, and violence.

A TBI is an injury to the brain caused by an external force, or trauma, to the head. The "external force" can be a direct blow, such as hitting the floor in a fall or striking the steering wheel in a car accident.

Even when the skull is not damaged or fractured, the force can injure the brain in a number of ways. The brain floats inside the skull in cerebrospinal fluid, and the rapid acceleration and deceleration of the head can shake the brain against the opposite sides of the skull. A common example is the head being whipped back and forth in a car accident.

Closed head injuries often do not show up on standard MRI or CT scans, because the damage can be spread out rather than limited to one area. When the nerve tissue of the brain is injured, it stops working correctly. That is why much of the diagnosis depends on you telling your car accident chiropractor about every symptom, so they can make a clinical diagnosis.

Not all TBIs are equally serious; they range from devastating to subtle. Even a minor brain injury, however, can have significant, lasting consequences.

## Common symptoms of a traumatic brain injury

A traumatic brain injury can be fatal. For survivors, TBI can cause a number of physical, cognitive and emotional symptoms.

### Physical symptoms

- Seizures of all types
- Muscle spasticity
- Double vision, blurred vision or low vision, even blindness
- Loss of smell or taste
- Speech problems, such as slow or slurred speech
- Headaches or migraines
- Fatigue, an increased need for sleep, and balance problems

### Cognitive symptoms

- Short-term or long-term memory loss
- Slower processing of information
- Trouble concentrating or paying attention for periods of time
- Difficulty keeping up with a conversation, or trouble finding words
- Spatial disorientation
- Problems with organization and impaired judgment
- Trouble doing more than one thing at a time, starting activities, or finishing tasks without reminders

### Emotional symptoms

- Increased anxiety
- Depression and mood swings
- Impulsive behavior
- Being more easily agitated, and difficulty seeing how your behavior affects others

## Get checked, even after a minor collision

If you or a loved one has been in a motor vehicle collision in Glendale, no matter how slight you think the damage is, it is important to get a proper examination and workup to rule out a concussion or closed head injury.
