## Symptoms and treatment of whiplash after a Glendale car accident

The top three auto accident injuries in Glendale, Arizona are whiplash or neck pain, closed head injury resulting in headache, and soft tissue injury. Let's take a close look at each type of injury, with some important tips on getting medical and chiropractic treatment after a car accident.

## Is whiplash a reality or a myth?

Whiplash is not a formal medical term. It is an informal description of an injury to the soft tissues of the neck: the muscles, tendons, ligaments, nerves and discs. Whiplash typically includes injuries to the joints between the neck vertebrae (called facets), the discs in the neck, and the nerve roots that leave the spinal cord between the vertebrae.

Whiplash is caused by the sudden deceleration, or rapid slowing, of a vehicle in an accident. This instant deceleration makes your head "whip" back and forth: your neck is forced through a sudden backward and forward movement. The medical name for it is a flexion-extension injury.

## Symptoms of whiplash after a motor vehicle accident

Because whiplash covers many different types of injury, it can cause many different symptoms. Common whiplash symptoms include neck and upper back pain or stiffness, shoulder pain or stiffness, headache, dizziness, and sometimes a burning feeling in the neck, shoulders or arms. Car accidents that cause severe whiplash can also cause memory problems, difficulty concentrating, trouble sleeping, fatigue and sometimes even depression.

Symptoms of whiplash often start right after the accident, and our chiropractic physicians warn that the pain usually gets worse over time: the fourth or fifth day after a car accident is classically the worst. Most people get out of the car feeling some sort of twinge in their neck, but it is not unusual to feel fine right after a car accident and have no whiplash symptoms for up to four days. Whiplash injuries do not heal quickly, and even when you are pain-free, the injured tissues can heal the wrong way. Less pain, or no pain, does not mean you don't have whiplash.

If you have neck pain after a car accident, get chiropractic treatment as quickly as possible. If the pain is severe, see a doctor or go to the hospital right away.

### Headache

Many people have a slight headache after a car accident even if they didn't hit their head on anything. Your head and neck were simply not built to tolerate a sudden stop from 20 or 30 mph, or even 5 mph, to zero. Car accident headaches can trick you by going away quickly, but some do not. If a headache lasts more than a day or two after a car accident, you should definitely seek chiropractic attention. It's better to know whether it is nothing at all or a sign of a more significant injury: a [closed head injury](/headaches-closed-head-injury/) may have occurred.

### Soft tissue injury: sprains, strains, bumps and bruises

The soft tissues of the body are its muscles, ligaments, tendons, discs and nerves. Ligaments attach bones to bones, and tendons connect muscles to bones. A soft tissue injury is a bruise, an abnormal stretch or a tear of the soft tissue. A contusion is the medical term for a bruise. In medical terms, you strain a muscle or a tendon, but you sprain a ligament or a joint.

Symptoms of soft tissue injury include muscle aches and pain, nerve pain, numbness or tingling, joint pain, and decreased or painful range of motion and stiffness in the injured part of the body. These symptoms can develop right after the accident or over several days, weeks or even months. Think of a twisted or sprained ankle: many people who have badly sprained an ankle say it was worse than a broken bone because it hurt so much and took so long to heal, and people who never rehabilitated a bad sprain often still call it "my weak ankle."

## Please don't delay getting care after a car accident

We cannot emphasize this enough. The most dangerous words you can say after a car accident are "maybe it will go away on its own." If you even think you have an injury, pain or other symptoms after a car accident, seek chiropractic attention right away. If the pain right after the accident is severe, go to the emergency room. But remember that the ER doctor is concerned only with life-threatening injuries: neither the ER nor a walk-in "doc in a box" clinic is meant to provide long-term treatment for car accident injuries. Otherwise, make an appointment with our office as soon as possible.

And know this: if you are in a car accident and do not get chiropractic or medical attention soon afterward, insurance companies will assume you weren't really injured, or weren't hurt as badly as you say. So always get the chiropractic and medical care you need after a car accident. Otherwise you may miss the chance to have your care paid for by the car insurance you pay for every month.
