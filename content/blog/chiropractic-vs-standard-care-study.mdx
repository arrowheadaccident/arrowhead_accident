## What the 2013 study compared

In a 2013 study by Goertz et al., patients were allowed to seek standard medical care without restriction, which meant they could see their medical doctors as they normally would for lower back pain.

> "Standard care included any or all of the following: a focused history and physical examination, diagnostic imaging as indicated, education about self-management including maintaining activity levels as tolerated, pharmacological management with the use of analgesics and anti-inflammatory agents, and physical therapy and modalities such as heat/ice and referral to a pain clinic" (p. 628).

On the chiropractic side, the authors wrote:

> "Treatments consisted of HVLA manipulation as the primary approach in all cases, with ancillary treatments at the doctor's discretion, including brief massage, the use of ice or heat in the lumbar area, stretching exercises, McKenzie exercises, advice on activities of daily living, postural/ergonomic advice; and mobilization" (p. 628).

HVLA stands for high-velocity, low-amplitude manipulation, also known as a chiropractic adjustment. This matters because chiropractors view the spine as an entire system, and both the approach to patients and the therapy are different.

## Should you see a chiropractor after a car accident?

This study of active-duty US military personnel with acute low back pain found that adding chiropractic care, with therapies such as heat, ice, stretching and specific exercises, to standard medical care gave better results than standard medical care alone, which included pain and anti-inflammatory medication, physical therapy and self-care advice. The study did not look at car accident injuries, but [low back pain](/low-back-pain/) is a common complaint after a crash.

The authors reported:

> "The results of our pragmatic pilot study indicate a statistically and clinically significant benefit to those receiving chiropractic manipulative therapy in addition to standard medical care" (p. 631).

And finally:

> "The results of this trial suggest that chiropractic, in conjunction with standard medical care, offers a significant advantage for decreasing pain and improving physical functioning compared with only standard medical care, for active-duty men and women between 18 and 35 years of age with acute low back pain when delivered in a pragmatic treatment setting" (p. 633).

This agrees with an earlier study by Liliedahl et al. (2010), which found lower overall costs for low back pain when care started with a chiropractor rather than a medical doctor.

## How we apply this after a car accident

Our doctors apply these protocols to motor vehicle accident patients every day. Many patients do not feel pain right after a car collision. In our experience, pain from underlying injuries often shows up on the fourth or fifth day after the crash; at the time of the accident, people are often too stressed to notice pain, as adrenaline takes over.

If you are in a lot of pain, we refer you to a [medical doctor](/pain-management/) to see whether medication would help, and chiropractic care continues while you take it.

If you or a loved one has been in an auto accident in the greater Phoenix area, it is a wise choice to visit a chiropractor for an exam and, if needed, specific car accident treatment.
