*This article provides general information, not legal advice.*

After a car accident, it can be difficult to separate useful information from assumptions about injuries and insurance. Here are six common misconceptions.

## Myth 1: An injury must be visible to be real

Pain and soft tissue injuries are not always visible. Symptoms should be assessed through a medical history and examination, with further testing when appropriate. Neither appearance nor a stereotype about accident claims is a substitute for an individual assessment.

Describe your symptoms accurately, including when they began and how they affect daily activities. This helps your clinician assess your condition and document your care.

## Myth 2: Every accident claim requires an attorney

Whether you need legal representation depends on your circumstances. A personal injury attorney can explain liability, insurance questions and settlement terms. Medical professionals assess and treat injuries; attorneys advise on legal rights and claims.

If you already have an attorney, our clinic can provide treatment records and discuss [billing under a lien](/no-cost-for-your-treatment-ever/). Ask about the terms that apply to your case.

## Myth 3: Chiropractic treatment guarantees a larger settlement

Treatment should be based on your clinical needs, not an expected insurance payment. A chiropractor can assess musculoskeletal symptoms such as [neck pain](/neck-chiropractor-arrowhead-az/) and [lower back pain](/low-back-pain/) and recommend appropriate care or a referral.

Accurate records can document injuries, treatment and any ongoing limitations. They do not guarantee coverage, compensation or a particular settlement amount.

## Myth 4: Every injury heals within 6 to 12 weeks

Recovery varies with the injury and the person. Some symptoms improve within weeks, while others persist and need further assessment. No single timetable applies to every patient.

Older studies illustrate that persistent symptoms are possible. Watkinson and colleagues followed 35 patients with neck soft tissue injuries for an average of 10.8 years; 86% still reported symptoms. Hildingsson and Toolanen followed 93 car accident patients for two years and reported work-related limitations in 43%. These findings describe those study groups, not the expected outcome for every person with [whiplash](/whiplash-injury/).

## Myth 5: Minor vehicle damage rules out an injury

Vehicle damage alone cannot determine whether someone is injured or how severe an injury is. An assessment should consider symptoms, examination findings and the circumstances of the collision. Research on whiplash has examined why simple comparisons between vehicle damage and injury can be misleading.

## Myth 6: No immediate pain means no injury

Some symptoms appear later. Changes in activity, inflammation and the body's response to a collision can affect when a person notices pain. The timing of symptoms alone does not establish or rule out an injury.

Report new or worsening symptoms to a qualified clinician. Do not assume that a delayed symptom is harmless, or that it is necessarily caused by the accident without an assessment.

## Understand your care and any proposed settlement

Keep accurate records and ask your clinician about your recovery and any likely future care. If you have legal questions, ask an attorney how a proposed settlement may affect your rights.
