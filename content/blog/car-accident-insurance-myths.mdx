Here are a few of the "insurance mantras" and myths about car accident victims and the professionals who help them.

## Mantra #1: Swindlers, pretenders and phonies

*Myth: Accident victims who say they were injured in a car accident are pulling a fast one, looking to hit the personal injury lottery.*

The overwhelming majority of people who say they were injured in a car accident **have actually been injured**. Insurance companies have worked hard to suggest, imply and sometimes even state that most people who claim an auto accident injury are committing fraud. Movies haven't helped either, with some actor wearing a neck brace and faking an injury. Yes, there are people who abuse the system to get a quick payout.

However, the vast majority of people who say they were injured in an auto accident really were. Having injuries and feeling pain after a crash is normal, par for the course, and not questionable. When two vehicles collide, thousands of pounds of steel smash into each other and the people inside are caught in the middle.

It is very hard to fake an injury in front of an experienced car accident chiropractor, who can usually tell a real injury from a fake one during an examination. Fakers are not the rule. They stick out like a sore thumb, and our office has turned would-be patients like that away.

## Mantra #2: Attorneys are ambulance chasers

*Myth: Attorneys who specialize in car accident cases are ambulance chasers who willingly work with fraudulent clients to exploit the system.*

Every profession has its bad actors, and it would be naive to pretend there are no unscrupulous attorneys. But they don't last in this type of practice. Make no mistake: attorneys earn money on auto accident cases, and the higher the settlement, the more they make. More importantly, the higher the settlement, the more the accident victim receives.

In our experience, most attorneys care about their clients and act in their best interest. The best attorneys only accept clients who are actually injured, and expect them to get whatever health care they feel is best for their injuries. The good attorneys work with chiropractors who understand the complexities of car crash injuries and how to document them, so the attorney can negotiate a settlement that covers all your expenses, including medical, direct and indirect costs. If you need an attorney, just let us know.

## Mantra #3: Everyone knows you go to the chiropractor to make a fast dollar

*Myth: If you have been in an accident, everyone knows to go to an accident and injury chiropractor because they can "print money" off your insurance claim.*

Say you were in a car accident, hit your head on the steering wheel, and chipped or broke one or more teeth. A dentist would be the logical choice, right? After you see your dentist and have your teeth fixed, the insurance company typically offers a settlement to pay your dental bills. So how is this different from seeing a chiropractor for neck and back injuries and pain?

This myth exists only because of the kinds of injuries doctors of chiropractic treat. Our chiropractic injury exams and treatment protocols are designed specifically for injuries to joints, ligaments, muscles and nerves (musculoskeletal and neurological injuries). These are the tissues most often injured in car accidents. It is unusual for a patient to break their teeth in a car accident; it is very common to have neck, shoulder, arm or leg pain and even lower back pain.

Chiropractors are the health care providers of choice for these injuries because they are the injuries we are trained to treat. Car accident chiropractors invest time and tuition in specialized training for injuries from motor vehicle accidents.

Typically, when your case closes, the insurance company should rightfully and legally reimburse your medical expenses and pay an additional settlement for pain and suffering. As chiropractors, we have little to do with that process. Our job is done when we have treated your injuries and helped your body heal to its maximum level of improvement. But your car accident chiropractor absolutely must document every aspect of your injuries, including any residual problems that may never heal completely.

## Mantra #4: My injuries will heal on their own within 6 to 12 weeks

*Myth: Car accident injuries are only sprain/strain injuries and always resolve on their own within 6 to 12 weeks.*

Insurance adjusters love to repeat the myth that car accident injuries heal on their own in 6 to 12 weeks. In fact, it is an insurance adjuster's mantra, even though adjusters have no training or expertise in medical matters. Many people believe it and pass it on to friends, family and coworkers, which keeps the myth alive. That is the reason for this article.

The research points the other way: injuries like these often last a long time and frequently never fully go away. According to Watkinson and Gargan (1), after an average of 10.8 years, 86% of people who had a motor vehicle injury still had related pain, and 68% eventually showed spinal degenerative changes on x-ray. Hildingsson and Toolanen (2) found that two years after a car accident neck injury, 43% of patients had discomfort severe enough to interfere with their ability to work.

1. Watkinson A, Gargan MF, Bannister GC. Prognostic factors in soft tissue injuries of the cervical spine. *Injury*. 1991 Jul;22(4):307–309.
2. Hildingsson C, Toolanen G. Outcome after soft-tissue injury of the cervical spine. A prospective study of 93 car-accident victims. *Acta Orthop Scand*. 1990 Aug;61(4):357–359.

## Mantra #5: No significant damage to the car means no serious injuries

*Myth: A car accident can only cause injuries in direct proportion to the damage to the car, so you must be faking your injuries to make money.*

This is simply not true. Studies done about 40 years ago showed mostly the opposite. According to Martinez (1), an 8 mph rear-end collision accelerates the car at two times the force of gravity (2g), while the occupant's head experiences 5g of acceleration. At 8 miles an hour. McNab (2) showed that a 15 mph crash accelerates the car to 10g and the occupant's head and neck to 20 to 25g. That is whiplash. In the 40 years of research since, no direct link has been found between the amount of damage to a car and the severity of injury (3).

This does not contradict what we say elsewhere about getting legal representation. In the real world, attorneys often hesitate to take a case when the damage estimate is below $1,500, because they know little money is likely to come from it and the case may not be worth their time.

1. Martinez J, Garcia D. "A Model for Whiplash." *J Biomech*. 1968;1:23.
2. McNab I. "Acceleration Extension Injuries of the Cervical Spine." In: Rothman RH, Simeone FA, eds. *The Spine*, vol. II. 1975.
3. Freeman MD, Croft AC, Rossignol AM, Weaver DS, Reiser M. A review and methodological critique of the literature refuting whiplash syndrome. *Spine*. 1999 Jan 1;24(1):86–96.

## Mantra #6: If you don't feel pain right away, you weren't hurt that badly

*Myth: If accident victims don't feel pain within the first 72 hours after a car accident, they aren't really injured.*

Accident victims usually do feel pain within the first 72 hours, but not always. Quite often it takes one to two weeks before you feel all of your symptoms.

Why? First, inflammation builds up over time. Most car accident victims "take it easy" the first week after an accident. If their neck hurts when they turn to the left, they simply stop turning to the left. Then, weeks or months later, when they go back to their normal activities, they suddenly find that fairly normal activities cause a great deal of pain. The injury was there all along; you just didn't know it. Chiropractors call these "underlying injuries."

Researchers also describe the "pain gating" principle: your nervous system and brain are wired to notice your most intense pain over lesser pain. Once your most severe injuries calm down, you start to notice other injured areas. Those injuries were there the whole time, but your brain didn't register them because the pain in one area, say your neck, was so much worse than in another, like your mid back.

## Don't settle too soon

It is very important not to settle your case until you know how much your body has healed, whether you will have lasting health problems, and especially whether you have any permanent injury. Remember that insurance adjusters are trained to get you to sign off or settle your case for as little money as possible.

That is why, after a car accident, whether or not you think you are hurt, you should see a chiropractor or medical doctor trained to diagnose and treat your injuries, and, just as important, a personal injury attorney. Your attorney can keep the insurance company at bay while you heal, protect your right to have your health restored to where it was before the accident, and pursue compensation for your pain and suffering.
